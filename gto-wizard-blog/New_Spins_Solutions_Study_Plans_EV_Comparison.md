****We have completely overhauled the Spin & Go solutions!****  
__These were solved to an eye-watering accuracy of 0.1%–0.017% of the pot__
We’ve added a new **Spin & Go + Ante format** that uses a 0.17bb ante per player, with 16bb starting stacks. This was among the most requested solutions from our Spin community!
Next, we **replaced the General solutions with ultra-high accuracy solutions!** These were solved to an eye-watering accuracy of 0.1%-0.017% of the pot. Finally, we’ll upload Test preflop-only Spin & Go solutions that contain many more sizes! These were used to research our new Spin trees and can be used to determine how to respond to almost any preflop actions!
**To recap the Spin & Go changes:**

  * New General solutions feature ultra-high accuracy and optimized bet sizes.

****EV Comparison****
The EV comparison feature is a powerful tool you can access in the Solution browser to understand how different hands in your range prefer to play! You can access this feature in the {{tooltip-title: strategy dropdown menu}} of the Solution browser. This function compares the expected value of two decisions and color-codes your range to display which hands are close, and which strongly prefer one action over the other.
{{tooltip-content: strategy dropdown menu}}
![New Spins solutions, Study Plans & EV Comparison](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/new-spins-solutions-study-plans-and-ev-comparison-article-image-5.jpg)
{{/tooltip-content}}
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/04/new-spins-solutions-study-plans-and-ev-comparison-article-image-7.jpg)
As an illustration, in the above settings, I’ve chosen to compare “Call” and Fold”. Hands that have higher EV as a call are shown in {{color: green}}green{{/color}}. Hands that have higher EV as a fold are shown in {{color: red}}red{{/color}}. Hands that are very close to the same EV are shown in white. You can also choose to compare the “Best Action”. This effectively shows the **regret** or EV loss of choosing one action against the opponent’s fixed strategy.
For example, on this Q96 board, the BTN has bet 73% pot. We can see at a glance which hands strongly prefer calling (green) over folding (red) and which hands are close (white). Hovering over **97** s, we observe that we should certainly continue when we hold the backdoor diamonds, but without that it’s a very marginal decision. Comparing EV is a great way to study value and continuation thresholds!
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/04/new-spins-solutions-study-plans-and-ev-comparison-article-image-6.jpg)
****Drill Close Decisions****
Practice tricky spots! Enabling this setting means you’ll only be dealt hands that have a close decision on their first action. A “close decision” is defined as a hand that has a similar EV between checking/betting or folding/continuing.
Examples:
  * Practicing your opening strategy you’d be dealt hands that are close between opening and folding.
  * Facing a bet you’d be dealt hands that are close between folding and continuing.
  * Practicing c-bets you’d be dealt hands that are close between checking and betting.

To use this feature, open a new training session and select **All Settings > Hands > Close Decisions**.

****View Pot Odds in Study Mode****
We’ve improved Study mode and Trainer by adding pot odds! Pot odds tell you what portion of the pot you need to win to justify a call [(learn more)](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-are-pot-odds-in-poker/).
This metric is great for deciding what hands are strong enough to call. Pot odds are displayed in the infobox of the Study mode **and next to the pot in the Trainer**.

****Drill Relative Stack Size****
For the MTT enthusiasts, we’ve added a new setting to the Trainer that allows you to define the hero’s stack as either a big, mid, or shortstack relative to the rest of the field. This allows you to play strategically similar stack depths without choosing the exact spots! The stack depths are defined as follows:
{{grid: 2}}
  * **Bigstack** > 133% of the average tournament stack.
  * **Midstack** is between 133% and 66% of the average tournament stack.
  * **Shortstack** < 66% of the average tournament stack.

![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/01/image-39.png)
{{/grid}}
To practice these stack sizes, open the trainer settings and select an MTT solution with mixed stacks. Under the Game section, you’ll find the option to select your relative stack depth.
****Squeeze Spots Shortcut****
{{grid: 2}}
Our previous Trainer settings didn’t allow you to filter for squeeze spots – you had to define the actions manually. So we’ve added two shortcuts to help you practice these spots!
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/01/image-40.png)
{{/grid}}
  * **Vs Raise-Call** means someone has opened, and another player has called. These spots give you the opportunity to squeeze (or overcall in some spots).
  * **Vs Squeeze** means you’re the player facing the squeeze, either as the preflop opener or caller.

We’ve added the ability to duplicate drills! This function is useful when you want to build several drills with similar settings while only changing a few variables. From the [Drills Management Page](https://app.gtowizard.com/practice/drills?ffdf_drill_type=my_drills&ref=blog.gtowizard.com), select this button to duplicate drills.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/01/image-41.png)
For example, once you’ve got everything set up the way you like it, you can duplicate one spot and then only change one variable at a time such as the positional pairs or stack depth. This is a great way to build your own training regiment!
