## **Tips for Studying GTO Solutions**
GTO can be intimidating to those who are new. Solutions are complex, and there’s just so much data! So how can one go about turning these charts into workable strategies?
{{width: 75%}}
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/image-130.png)
{{/width}}
### **Study Ranges, Not Hands!**
Trying to memorize every hand is impossible. Poker is simply far too complex. Instead of trying to mix every frequency correctly, focus on the overall strategy! The goal is to understand 'why' rather than 'what' the solver is doing.
### **Focus on Thresholds**
Learning basic thresholds is one of the fastest ways to become a solid poker player. Which hand classes are reasonable stack-offs in this spot? What is the strongest hand class that starts to mix folds here? What’s the weakest hand that can value bet here?
Thresholds guide and sculpt your strategy. Instead of trying to memorize every hand individually, you should focus on things like the bottom of your continuation range. 
For example, in this spot (CO vs BB SRP, [CO bets 33% pot on JJ4](https://app.gtowizard.com/solutions?gametype=Cash6m500zGeneral&depth=100&history_spot=8&preflop_actions=F-F-R2.3-F-F-C&soltab=strategy&board=JhJd4d&flop_actions=X-R1.7&ref=blog.gtowizard.com)), we should note that a made hand never folds. Open the ["Filters" tab](https://help.gtowizard.com/study-mode/?ref=blog.gtowizard.com#!/filters) in the Solution browser and look for {{color: blue}}blue lines{{/color}} which indicate {{color: blue}}folds{{/color}}. This is the bottom of your continuation range.
{{width: 55%}}
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/how-to-become-a-gto-wizard-image-4.png)
{{/width}}
### **Study Hand Classes**
One useful study technique is to break down the strategy into different hand classes using filters. Then, compare strategically similar boards and review different hand classes to develop an intuition about their strategy. You can also use the ["Breakdown" tab](https://help.gtowizard.com/study-mode/?ref=blog.gtowizard.com#!/breakdown_tab) (press 3 in the Solution browser) to see how different hands behave. You’ll see similar patterns on a lot of boards after a while.
### **Think About What You’re Targeting With Your Bet Size**
{{grid: 3fr 1fr}}
Every bet you make targets some part of your opponent’s range. By “target,” we mean those hands are [indifferent](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-three-laws-of-indifference/) between calling and folding in the face of your bet.  
  
The hands you target determine your value-bet thresholds. Consider how to get value and what sizing makes sense to target those parts of their range.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/image-131.png)
{{/grid}}
{{question-mark}}
So how do we know what hands we’re targeting with a bet?
{{/question-mark}}
Well, there is only one place that makes logical sense to look.  
That's right! Look into the response of the player who a shot/bet is being fired at.
### **Look Into Villain’s Response**
It’s important to look into Villain’s strategy to see how they _should_ respond. Then, ask yourself if their response is _realistic_ and what adjustments you might make against different _deviations_.
Looking at their response helps you to understand why the solution works in the first place, [understand blockers](https://blog.gtowizard.com/understanding-blockers-in-poker/), and think through your strategy.
### **Use Aggregated Reports To Study Trends**
Rather than studying one board at a time, you can study [aggregated reports](https://help.gtowizard.com/aggregate-reports-guide/?ref=blog.gtowizard.com#!/aggregated_reports). Apply filters to study strategically similar flops and look for general trends in the strategy at different points on the flop.
Ask yourself: “What sizing does it _generally_ use on low paired flops in this spot?” and “Why that is?”
Try to develop a broader heuristic that explains the _cause→effect relationship_.
You can also study [turn reports](https://help.gtowizard.com/aggregate-reports-guide/?ref=blog.gtowizard.com#!/aggregated_turn_reports) for one particular flop. They're great for developing _follow-through strategies_.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/how-to-become-a-gto-wizard-image-6-1.png)
### **Use the Ranges Tab To Understand the Big Picture**
The ["Ranges" tab](https://help.gtowizard.com/study-mode/?ref=blog.gtowizard.com#!/ranges_tab) lets you view both player’s ranges at the same time. Comparing range asymmetries and equity distributions can be used to understand the motivations behind the GTO strategy. You can compare different hand classes and equity buckets.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/how-to-become-a-gto-wizard-image-7.png)
## **Fundamental Poker Math**
{{grid: 4fr 1fr}}
**Poker is intuitive in practice, but mathematical in nature.** Humans cannot calculate optimal strategies at the table. However, it’s imperative to learn these fundamental concepts so that you can apply them in an abstract sense.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/03/image-134.png)
{{/grid}}
You should understand that you don’t need to defend wide when your opponent puts in more money relative to the pot. You should understand the basic shapes of ranges and how that influences your strategy. You should know the principle of [indifference](https://blog.gtowizard.com/the-three-laws-of-indifference/). You should learn how [pot odds](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-are-pot-odds-in-poker/) connect to the required bluffing frequency.
****The goal with poker math is to understand the concepts on a broad, abstract level.****
  * [**Expected Value**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-is-expected-value-in-poker/) – Every decision you make is underpinned by one goal: to maximize return.
  * [**Equity**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-is-equity-in-poker/) – Equity refers to your chances of winning a hand if the pot were checked down.
  * [**Range Morphology**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/range-morphology/) – Ranges are easier to understand when you think of them as shapes.
  * [**ICM Basics**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/icm-basics/) – Learn how tournament survival pressure affects your strategy.
  * [**Visualizing Implied Odds**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/visualizing-implied-odds/) – The value you expect to gain on future streets when you outdraw your opponent.
  * [**What Are Pot Odds in Poker?**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/what-are-pot-odds-in-poker/) – This essential risk-reward calculation tells you when a call becomes profitable.
  * [**MDF & Alpha**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mdf-alpha/) – Minimum Defense Frequency is the shield⛊ you use to prevent your opponents from running you over with bluffs. Learn how wide to defend in poker.
  * [**Mathematical Misconceptions in Poker**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/mathematical-misconceptions-in-poker/) – Address common misunderstandings and develop a more nuanced understanding of these mathematical concepts.
  * [**How to Solve Toy Games**](https://blog.gtowizard.com/how-to-solve-toy-games/) – Put it all together by learning how to solve toy games.
