### Why Late-Stage Decisions Matter
Most MTT regs study chip EV strategies which are designed to accumulate chips rather than maximize $EV. [_In a previous exploration_](https://blog.gtowizard.com/theoretical-breakthroughs-in-icm/), we showed that chip EV strategies lose a significant amount of money in late stages, especially near the bubble and final table.
**_But why does that matter if late-stage tournament spots are less common?_**
As Andrew Brokos explains in [_Tournament Poker's Hidden Stakes_](https://blog.gtowizard.com/tournament_pokers_hidden_stakes/), your buy-in tells you what's at stake at the start, but that can grow enormously as you progress. By the final table, a single decision can involve tournament equity worth many times your original investment. That's why **late-stage skill can have such an outsized impact on your results**. You reach those spots less often, but once you do, o decision can be worth more than a month of regular grinding.

Picture this. You're a midstack on the biggest final table of your life. The short-stacked HJ opens 2bb, the BTN raises almost all of their chips, effectively all-in. You look down at pocket Jacks in the BB.
**_What's your play?_**
[![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/09/data-src-image-e5d9837c-589f-496f-87b9-ac806eaad057.png)](https://youtu.be/_IiFpKlPB1k?si=EtHakjvSaUFZMWk4&t=533&ref=blog.gtowizard.com)
That was Greg Mueller's decision at the 2026 WSOP Main Event, with $2.25 million already locked and $10 million up top.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/09/table-layout-light--3-.png)
Han Feng, the shortest stack, min-opens HJ with **A♦A♣**. In chip EV that's just an open-jam at 8bb. Under ICM, aces prefer the small raise, splitting off from the rest of the range.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/09/5-1.png)
Saaskilahti 3-bets 9♠9♥, leaving about 3bb behind. That "almost all in" sizing is a real ICM tactic: it keeps a fold available if a covering stack jams behind him. The problem is his hand. Feng's min-open has already polarized his range, so in theory 99 is too thin to 3-bet.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/09/data-src-image-57354589-b836-45af-87f8-ce058d6cd12f.png)
Then it's on Mueller in BB with Jacks. He's got 22bb and $2.25 million already in his pocket. Tough spot.
**He tank-folds.** [_PokerNews called it_](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/08/han-feng-busts-wsop-main-event-final-table-52068.htm?ref=blog.gtowizard.com) a brilliant fold. Let's check.
According to GTO, folding is the correct play, although it’s right on the cusp. Pocket jacks sit at the very edge of a narrow optimal shoving range, so both shoving and folding are viable options.
![](https://blog.gtowizard.com/content/2026/09/data-src-image-761204e5-19ec-45e5-9d56-0f448fa9e058.png)
Here's the part worth thinking about. How much tighter should you play in this spot with ICM pressure?
How much does the payout pressure affect this decision? In chip EV, jacks facing a min-open and a short 3-bet are a snap jam, the kind of decision that's so easy you have to remind yourself to fake hesitation. ICM took a rather easy spot and made it hard. Mueller was right to recognize that it was a close decision.

## 3-Way Postflop ICM Solving
Once your preflop spot is solved, **GTO Wizard AI** carries the ranges, stacks, and payouts straight into postflop, so the flop strategy you study is built on the exact preflop ranges you just solved.
A chip leader, a middle stack, and a short stack see a flop. The same bet puts each player under a different kind of pressure. **Now you can solve the whole interaction with ICM and bounties built in.**
A middle stack can face a difficult decision when losing the pot means busting before a shorter stack. A valuable bounty can make fighting for that same pot more attractive. And the player behind you can change which bets, calls, and raises are profitable before they've even acted. Understanding those competing incentives is a huge opportunity to separate yourself from players relying on chip EV intuition.
**Now you can test the details that decide these pots.** Compare the same flop under ICM and chip EV. Change who covers whom. Increase a bounty. ### Why Fold EV Isn't Always Zero
In a simple chip EV spot, if you fold, you win no chips, so we measure the value of other decisions relative to that option. But tournament utility is measured in tournament equity (ICM value), not chips. Survival is what matters.
**When you fold in a multiway spot, it can change the strategies of the players behind you, which in turn affects your tournament equity.** Those players can clash, knock each other out, or make mistakes that leak tournament equity to you. You can ladder without winning another chip. For example, say you are 1 of 3 players remaining, but the last 2 are maniacs in the blinds. You might be more willing to fold on the BTN to let them clash. Setting fold EV to 0 would hide that information.
On a more cerebral point, folding generates a card removal effect that can theoretically alter optimal strategy among the remaining active players, which in turn affects your tournament equity. This means that some hands actually generate more EV than others when folding, and again, setting that to some flat number like 0 is misleading.
After speaking to several pros, we settled on a fixed reference payoff: the tournament equity you'd have if you lost the pot and the remaining players split it. Every action gets measured against that same number, including folding.
