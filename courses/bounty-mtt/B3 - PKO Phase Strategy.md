# B3 — PKO Phase Strategy

## Overview
Bounty EV is not constant across a PKO — it peaks early and depletes steadily. Phase awareness tells you WHEN to press and when to tighten. The format rewards a specific operational plan: play from the start, race to cover, and gamble intelligently while bounties are rich.

## Key Concepts
- **Bounties are a depleting resource.** Early: starting bounties are a significant portion of the buy-in, ICM pressure is minimal, untouched bounties are at maximum. Every elimination removes a starting bounty from circulation; mounting ICM pressure reduces the relative value of marginal chip gains. Individual bounties grow in dollars, but the total pool of available bounty EV shrinks.
- **The late-stage reversal:** only at the very end does the dynamic partially reverse — the eventual winner claims the accumulated bounty on their own head. The final table's biggest bounty (usually the chip leader's) is worth contesting.
- **There is a race to become a covering stack:** covering stacks enjoy negative risk premiums and realize bounty EV more efficiently. Building the covering stack early compounds.
- **Chip value of bounties trends downward** — the monetary value of chips rises faster than the monetary value of progressive bounties. Late-tournament bounties matter less relative to ICM. [GTO Wizard]
- **Registration is an EV decision:** in regular MTTs late registration can increase ROI; in PKOs it actively harms win rate.

## Phase Map
| Phase | Bounty dynamics 
| Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Early (start → ~first third)** | Peak bounty EV per chip; minimal ICM; everyone covers everyone on hand one | Play from the start; contest bounties; build the covering stack; widest correct gambles |
| **Mid (field halves)** | Pool depleting; stacks diverge; coverage now matters | Race to cover; pressure players you cover (especially to your right); tighten when covered |
| **Late (approaching money/FT)** | ICM risk premiums rise; bounties relatively diluted | Shift toward standard ICM play; bounty adjustments shrink; pick spots selectively |
| **Final table** | Reversal — accumulated heads are huge; winner keeps own head | Contest the big heads; winner-take-most bounty dynamics on the leader's scalp |

## The Three Key Tactics (BBZ)

### 1. Always play from the start
- Late registration actively harms your win rate in PKOs: every knockout removes bounty EV from the ecosystem. Entering late = paying full price for a tournament with less bounty EV remaining.
- Re-entry rules: only re-enter while early — large field, many starting bounties in play, average stack still near starting stack. In small fields or once stacks have diverged, re-entering is hard to justify.

### 2. Play aggressively — especially when covering
- There is a race to become a covering stack.
- Requires: pressuring shorter stacks, widening value thresholds, embracing variance in profitable all-in spots.
- Once you cover a table segment — especially players to your right — apply relentless pressure.
- Conversely, when short and covered, tighten (see B2 Bucket C).

### 3. Gamble early, gamble intelligently
- PKOs make marginal stack-offs more forgiving: the bounty often compensates for thinner equity margins.
- In regular ICM, the better mistake is stacking off too tight. In PKOs, the better mistake is often stacking off too wide — especially early.
- Not blind gambling: recognize when the bounty justifies the variance. Study off-table; in-game, err slightly toward aggr
ession.

## Decision Rules
1. **Never late-reg a PKO** unless the field is huge and the bounty pool is demonstrably intact.
2. **Early:** apply B1 math with the simple conversion; take the wide end of every marginal all-in decision where you cover.
3. **Mid:** track who YOU cover. Your aggression budget is allocated to players you cover — especially those to your right (they act before you every hand).
4. **Late:** recompute Bounty Power; if it has fallen materially, shift toward standard ICM play (the equity drop no longer pays for the risk premium).
5. **Final table:** price the leader's head into your ranges; remember the winner's-own-head reversal makes survival at the very top worth more than mid-ICM suggests.

## Common Leaks
- **Late registering or excessive re-entries** — paying full price for a depleted bounty pool.
- **Playing early levels tight "to survive"** — you're forfeiting the format's peak EV window.
- **Applying early-stage aggression late** — as ICM rises and bounties dilute, the same gambles become torches.
- **Pressuring players who cover YOU** — the negative risk premium doesn't apply; you're just spewing.
- **Ignoring the direction of the bounty pool** — bounties on eliminated players' heads left the ecosystem; update or overcall.
- **Stopping at "be aggressive"** — aggression without coverage math is just spew with extra steps.

## Heuristics
- "Bounty EV peaks on hand one and depletes from there"
- "Race to cover; then pressure everyone to your right"
- "Late reg = paying full price for less product"
- "Too wide early beats too tight; too tight late beats too wide"
- "The winner's head is the last big bounty — final table reversal"

## Quiz Spots
- Tournament starts in 10 minutes, you can play → register from the start (peak bounty EV window)
- You bust at level 3; field is large; average stack ~1.5x starting → re-enter (conditions met)
- Mid-stage, you cover the two players to your right with average bounties → widen opens/3-bets against t
hem specifically
- Late, 2 tables left, average bounty has halved relative to stacks → tighten toward standard ICM
- Final table, chip leader has 40% of chips and the biggest head → your shove-calling range vs them widens materially
