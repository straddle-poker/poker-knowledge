## What Are Poker Preflop Charts?
Poker preflop charts are visual guides that show you which hands to play from every position at the table. They take the guesswork out of your most frequent decision in poker: should I play this hand or fold it?
Every hand of poker starts with the same question. A preflop chart gives you the answer before you even have to think about it. The chart tells you whether to raise, call, or fold based on your position (where you’re sitting relative to the button), your stack depth (how many big blinds you have), and the action in front of you (has someone already raised, 3-bet, or gone all-in?).
Modern preflop charts are generated by poker solvers — software that calculates the mathematically optimal strategy (known as GTO, or Game Theory Optimal) through billions of simulated hands. The resulting ranges represent the strategy that cannot be exploited by any opponent, regardless of how they play.

## Position-by-Position Range Preview
Here’s what GTO opening ranges look like at a typical MTT table with a 40bb effective stack:
Position | Open % | What This Means  
---|---|---  
UTG | ~17% | Tight. Only premium hands and strong suited connectors. You’re first to act with 5-6 players behind you.  
UTG +1 | ~19% | Slightly wider. A few more suited hands.  
UTG +2 | ~23% | Slightly wider. More suited hands and more broadways.  
Hijack (HJ) | ~28% | You can add more suited hands and pocket pairs as fewer players remain to act.  
Cutoff (CO) | ~36% | The range opens up significantly. More suited connectors, and weaker broadways become profitable.  
Button (BTN) | ~50% | Nearly half the deck. Position advantage postflop means you can profitably open much wider.  
Small Blind (SB) | ~83% | Very wide mix of raises and limps. Only fold the worst hands.  
These percentages shift dramatically based on stack depth. At 15bb, your UTG range tightens and becomes much more push-or-fold. At 100bb, it widens with more 3-bet/call possibilities. BBZ’s charts cover every stack depth from 5bb to 100bb so you always know the correct range.

What’s the difference between ChipEV and ICM charts?
ChipEV charts assume every chip is worth the same (standard early/mid tournament play). ICM charts factor in tournament payout structures, where your chip value changes based on how close you are to pay jumps. If you play tournaments, you need both — ChipEV for early stages and ICM for bubbles, final tables, and satellite play.
