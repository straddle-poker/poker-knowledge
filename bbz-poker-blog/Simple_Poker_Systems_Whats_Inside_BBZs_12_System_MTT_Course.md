## What are Poker Systems?
The premise is old and not complicated. A solver answer is exact, and it is exact about one board, one stack depth, one pair of ranges. What you want at the table is the thing that survives the turn card changing. So you take a spot that comes up constantly, work it out properly once, and come away with a rule you can run in a second and a half.
Do that twelve times and you have the [Simple Poker Systems](https://bbzpoker.com/product/simple-poker-systems/) course.
A student put the method better than the sales page does:
**Faulkdogg** : “Rather than trying to memorize outputs Jordan teaches you concepts that apply across a large number of situations. He then reinforces those concepts by drilling in real-time and comparing his answer to the solver output. You will immediately be able to use these systems at the table.”
What a system buys you is repeatability. It plays the same on hour six as on hour one, the same across six tables as across one, the same when you are card dead and irritated as when you are running good. Over a year that consistency is worth more than the last sliver of EV you would squeeze out by rebuilding every spot from scratch.

## How Jordan Builds a System
A solver will tell you exactly what to do in a spot, and that answer is worth having. It is also an answer about one board, one stack depth, one pair of ranges, and it arrives as a mix. Change the turn card and you get a different one. The value is in the pattern sitting underneath all of them, and reaching that pattern is the work.
So the work is in the repetition. Run the spot over enough boards and stack depths and the parts that hold separate out from the parts that move. Frequencies move around a lot. What is driving them tends to hold.
Whatever holds becomes the system, and it gets written in terms you can see while you are playing. Who has the range advantage on this texture. Whether the caller can credibly have the nuts. How many draws are out there for them to bluff with. You will recall a condition at the table long after the percentage is gone.
Then it gets checked back the other way. You play the rule against the solver and measure what the shortcut costs. The rules that give up almost nothing are the ones that make the course. That test is the reason a system is worth trusting at speed: it has already been priced against the exact answer, and it came back cheap.
## What You Get Back
A system closes the spot. Once BB versus SB limped pots is a rule you trust, it stops drawing study time, and it stops drawing table time, which is the half people undervalue. The seconds you are not spending there are seconds available somewhere that is still hard.
That is the compounding part. Ordinary study leaves you with an answer to a hand. A system leaves you with a rule for a whole class of hands, so the session you put in during March is still paying you in July.
Twelve of those and the shape of your study changes. You spend your time on what is still open, with the settled spots running themselves.
It is also why the runtimes fall where they do. Rivers take two hours forty-one of the ten, more than any other street, because river pots are the biggest and a rule you can trust there pays the most per hour you spend building it.
## What a System Looks Like: C-Bet Folding Flops
System 7 shows the shape these take, so here is the logic of one.
Most players treat c-betting as a hand problem. Look at your two cards, decide whether they are strong enough to bet, and if a raise comes back, work it out then. That last part is the leak. By the time the raise arrives you are solving a hard problem on a clock with money already in the middle.
The system flips the order. You decide whether you are bet-folding before you bet, and you decide it off the board, not your hand.
### High, Dry, Disconnected Boards
A-7-2 rainbow, K-8-3 rainbow, Q-6-2 rainbow. Your range as the preflop raiser is far stronger than the caller’s here, so you bet nearly everything at a small size, a quarter to a third of pot. When a raise comes back, it is rare and it is heavily weighted to value, because there are very few draws for the caller to bluff with. On these boards you fold everything below top pair to a raise. That includes second pair. It feels bad and it is correct: your small bet only needed to work often enough to pay for itself, and it did.
### Middling Connected Boards
9-8-6 two-tone, J-T-7, 7-6-5. This is where your range is weakest relative to the caller’s, because they have called preflop with exactly the hands that connect here and you have not. Bet far less often. But the hands you do bet, you defend. Raises on these boards are wide, draw-heavy and full of hands that will barrel a blank. Folding top pair here is the mirror-image error to calling second pair on A-7-2.
### Paired Boards
K-K-4, 8-8-3, 5-5-2. Almost nobody has trips. Raises are close to pure bluff at a low frequency. Bet wide and small, and do not fold any pair to a single raise.
None of that is exotic on its own. What makes it work is that the category gets named before you put money in, so the raise never becomes something you have to solve at the table.
The course spends 42 minutes on this one, because naming the category correctly is where the work is. The other eleven systems are built the same way.
